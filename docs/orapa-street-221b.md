# EN DRAPA STREET 221B

**14+** ages | **2-5** players | **30** min.

*What goes on inside Sherlock's mind when he makes a deduction? On the board, see if you can perfectly locate the deductive tools Sherlock uses, along with the positions and colors of the scattered clues. If we can solve this riddle, it will be just like solving a case as brilliantly as Sherlock himself—as we always have.*

---

### Components

2 Boards, 2 Stands, 4 Sets of Clue Tokens (or Clue Tiles), 2 Reference Sheets, Deduction Sheet

---

### Game Setup (2 Players)

Players take on the roles of both puzzle creator and detective. As a creator, a player places clue tokens on their deduction board and answers their opponent's questions based on their placement.

**Creator Setup :** Take 1 deduction board and set it up behind a stand so that its contents are hidden from your opponent. Take 1 set of clue tokens. In the standard game, freely select 6 out of the 13 total tokens to use. The 6 tokens chosen by both players must be identical and must include all 4 colors.

**Token Placement Rules :** Place the tokens on the deduction board according to the following rules:

a. All tokens must be placed so that their perimeters align perfectly with the grid points or grid lines inside the board.

b. Token perimeters may touch the outer edges of the board grid, but cannot extend past them. Tokens cannot share adjacent edges or vertices with each other. (i.e., multiple tokens cannot occupy the same grid space simultaneously).

c. A single token cannot be completely surrounded and obscured by other tokens when viewed from any edge of the grid.

d. Tokens cannot be placed in the dark-shaded central area.

**Detective Setup :** Prepare 1 deduction sheet and a pencil/pen (an eraser is recommended). A small set of clue tokens may be used as deduction aids. Determine the starting player, then take turns asking questions (as the detective) and giving answers (as the creator).

---

### Play!

On your turn, as the detective, you shoot a "brainwave" (laser) into your opponent's board from outside the grid edge. As the creator, your opponent inspects their board to trace the path of the brainwave and reveals two results:

(A) Where the brainwave exits the grid edge.  
(B) How the color of the brainwave changed.

The detective notes the answer on their deduction sheet in any format they prefer. Hearing the answer completes the current player's turn.

> ※ A detective may re-ask about a previously fired brainwave without using up their turn.

#### Example Dialogue:
* **A:** "If I fire a brainwave from **B H**, what color comes out?"  
  * **B:** "It exits at **H 13** in light purple."
* **A:** "If I fire a brainwave from **4 H**, what color comes out?"  
  * **B:** "It exits at **D 4** in purple."

---

### Firing Brainwaves & Color Rules

**Firing Brainwaves :** When fired, a brainwave can enter in 2 possible directions from a single point outside the grid. The detective selects a grid edge number (1–14) or letter (A–N), along with a direction—**Right (R)**, **Left (L)**, **Up (U)**, **Down (D)**, or **Horizontal (H)**—and asks the creator.

**Reflection :** Brainwaves travel in a straight line from the firing point into the grid and always reflect at a 60-degree angle whenever they strike a token.

**Color Changes :** There are four clue token colors: Red, Yellow, Blue, and White. A brainwave is initially colorless (transparent), but changes color upon striking clue tokens according to these rules:

a. When a brainwave strikes a White token, it turns White. Striking a Red token turns it Red, Blue turns it Blue, and Yellow turns it Yellow.
b. When it encounters Red and Yellow, it turns Orange. Red and Blue turn it Purple, and Yellow and Blue turn it Green. Encountering all three colors—Red, Yellow, and Blue—turns it Black.
c. Red and White make Pink, Yellow and White make Lemon, and Blue and White make Sky Blue. If it strikes all four colors—Red, Yellow, Blue, and White—the brainwave turns Grey.
d. Striking the same color multiple times counts as meeting that color only once (e.g., passing through Red twice still counts as 1 Red).

#### Colors Combination Guide:
* Red + Yellow = **Orange**
* Yellow + Blue = **Green**
* Red + Blue = **Purple**
* Red + Yellow + Blue = **Black**
* Red + White = **Pink**
* Blue + White = **Sky Blue**
* Yellow + White = **Lemon**
* Yellow + Blue + White = **Light Green**
* Black + White = **Grey**
* Red + Yellow + White = **Light Orange**
* Red + Blue + White = **Light Purple**

---

### Game End

The first player to correctly deduce the exact positions and states of all clue tokens wins. During their turn, a detective may submit their completed deduction sheet to the opponent. The creator checks the submitted sheet against their board. If the sheet matches the board's setup perfectly, the detective wins! If the submitted sheet differs even slightly from the actual board, the detective who submitted it loses immediately and the game ends.

If both players guess incorrectly once each, both lose.

---

### RULES FOR 3+ PLAYERS

For 3 or more players, designate 1 player as the Puzzle Creator. The creator sets up tokens on their deduction board but does not participate in the deduction competition; they only answer the detectives' questions. All other players act as detectives competing against each other. Detectives take turns asking questions and tracing clues.

The first detective to correctly identify the exact positions and placement of all tokens on the creator's board wins.

> ※ In games with 3+ players, each detective is allowed up to 2 submission attempts. If a detective guesses incorrectly once, they may try again on a future turn. A second incorrect submission eliminates that detective. If all detectives fail on their second attempt, everyone loses.

---

### Game Variants

* **Variant 1 - Sherlock Seven :** Once you are accustomed to probing Sherlock's mind, increase the difficulty! Place 1 additional token (7 total) on the board. Both players must use the exact same set of 7 tokens.
* **Variant 2 - Sherlock Extreme :** Use only 5 tokens on the board. In this mode, players do NOT need to have identical sets of tokens. All tokens are private.
